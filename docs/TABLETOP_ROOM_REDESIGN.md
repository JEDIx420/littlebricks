# LittleBricks — the real tabletop room and sourced-parts campaign

**Review date:** 2026-11-11. **Remote baseline:** `41e45b71fc7417433b65ca06475f06f24abd46ce` (previous LB-057–LB-067 campaign completed). **Status:** design and implementation instructions, not completed runtime work.

## Live browser observations and evidence limits

Actual user-shared Chrome tab `http://localhost:8080/`, desktop viewport approximately 1470 × 745, exercised from Welcome → Let's Build → Solo → setup → tabletop. Input was real pointer motion/click and camera dragging through browser controls; the page did not expose an export fingerprint tying it conclusively to remote HEAD. User-supplied screenshot independently shows a newer Antigravity browser viewport and the same design problems.

1. **Welcome:** functional and calmer than the former black screen, but cream page + generic centered form still lacks a toy-filled scene, playful motion, meaningful objects and a sense of place. Mode cards take a tall scrolling column on the desktop viewport; the setup form is monochrome and reads like a settings form.
2. **World:** a saturated mint green stud field fills nearly the whole viewport. A flat beige polygon behind it reads as a platform, not a physical timber table. There are no recognizable walls, floor, window, furniture, room lighting or table edges; the scene cannot communicate scale or family atmosphere.
3. **HUD:** black rectangular top strip, eleven vertically stacked dark rectangular buttons on right, and flat text-only part buttons across the bottom dominate the scene. Some rotation labels render a replacement/missing glyph. The all-important bricks are tiny visual elements in a sea of green studs and UI rectangles.
4. **Placement improved:** moving pointer from center to a distant board coordinate moved the selected 2×3 ghost there. This contradicts the *older* center-locked bug, which must not be reported as current. Selecting the 2×3 visible part worked.
5. **Camera partially works:** clicking Camera: Drag changed status to `Camera Mode Active (Left-drag orbits table)`. A mouse drag clearly orbited the board, even while a brick was selected. However, the control then read `Camera: Pan (C)` and the next click returned to normal placement; the test did **not** establish an intuitive lateral pan gesture or camera mode semantics. No safe persistent pan was demonstrated. Camera controls require user-facing discoverability and independent action tests.
6. **Placement confirmation ambiguous:** after returning to ordinary placement, canvas clicking and clicking `Place (Enter)` yielded no obvious accepted-event, counter, or feedback change in this short test. Do not conclude an engine bug solely from a static screenshot; run a deterministic current-export check with selected candidate validity, click/Enter, command result, hand count and event state recorded.
7. **Assets:** the sample part renders as a basic cuboid with studs; the user reports ship wheel, curved slope and glazing look wrong. The agent claims procedural specialty geometry on 126 entries, but **that is not proof of sourced, recognizable physical part shapes**. Audit renders, geometry provenance and silhouettes before calling these authentic.
8. **Audio/haptics:** visible `Sound: ON` existed at Welcome, but this remote browser instrumentation cannot listen to audio or feel device vibration. Those gates remain `not_run` in *this* review, regardless of source tests.

## Target art direction: one cozy room, one real table, one giant plate

Imagine a bright family craft room on a sunny afternoon. The **game world is one bounded room**, not a boundless sky. The **one playable construction surface** is a single large, standard-stud-grid baseplate centered on a finished wood table, with visible margins around every side. Room dressing is ambient and never a second build area.

- **Hero composition:** 32 × 32 stud baseplate (or one explicitly documented larger *single* plate if performance permits), a bevelled thin edge and realistic stud spacing, on a solid timber 1.25–1.6m rectangular family table. Plate should occupy about 55–70% of table top width in default three-quarter view so the wood and tray remain visible. Camera must never imply plate suspended in void.
- **Room composition:** enclosed floor, skirting, two softly colored walls with a corner for parallax; one sunny window with curtains, wall art, shelf with books/toys, rug, chair/stool. Props have correct human scale compared with the table. No random floating décor and no photo-uncanny clutter.
- **Lighting:** soft angled warm daylight from the window, controlled ambient fill and gentle contact shadows/ambient occlusion where supported. No bloom haze, glowing mint board, harsh black shafts, unstable temporal artifacts or eye-straining brightness; tune for Web Compatibility + Android.
- **Materials:** satin rather than emissive plastic; molded stud details; warm oak/birch wood with mild roughness/grain; daylight cream walls; accents in peach, teal and cornflower blue. Baseplate defaults to a friendly muted green or pale sand, not screen-filling neon. Color-contrast and reduced-motion variants required.
- **Camera:** room visible in wide establishing shot on entering; smooth push to working three-quarter view; user can always recover with Frame Plate. Orbit pivot on plate center; default tilt 35–60°, limits avoid under-table camera and wall clipping. Pan translates target along tabletop plane with bounded drift; zoom dollies within limits and preserves plate readability. Room background may simplify at close zoom.
- **Physical selection:** 2–4 shallow little **parts trays/buckets on the visible table margins** with geometry previews; colored cup rim, selected-piece lift, subtle toss/rattle and inventory amount. Tap a tray to expand a compact categorized drawer with **real rotating part thumbnails** (not names-only buttons). Random six-piece hand must present actually held pieces in Pass & Play/Mixed Bags; Free Build can show full catalog. Collapse tray gracefully when zoomed, occluded, on phone, or screen-reader accessed. Never make part selection depend on an occluded 3D object; keep an accessible overlay fallback.
- **Minimal HUD:** top left small creation name, player/turn and timer, top right unobtrusive icon controls for undo, menu, finish; compact bottom-right rotate/place/cancel cluster shown only when holding. Big text buttons only as touch accessible expansion; avoid eleven always-visible black buttons. Status messages short, friendly and transient. Clear held piece and snap validity tint that doesn't depend on color alone.
- **Opening experience:** a short branded opening camera vignette in the same room (3–5s skippable) then 3 inviting mode cards with artwork using **real scene captures**; no blank marketing page. Theme choice should change room palette, table props and curated brick bags, not replace the coherent room shell.

### Two-layer architecture

1. **Environment layer:** authored room assembled from licensed assets, table, trays, fixed camera/light rig, sky outside window. Static collision where necessary only. Excluded from save and building physics.
2. **Build layer:** existing canonical studs/plate lattice, eligible buildable part meshes, connector metadata, held ghost, accepted event list. **World-space transforms identical** across tabletop rendering, placement, save, networking, explorer and film. Environment never shifts part coordinate origin or violates PC-01–PC-10.

Stage this as a reusable room scene `game/client/scenes/room_tabletop.tscn` with isolated scene configuration and resource paths. Main scene instantiates it; do not rewrite engine or networking core. Minifigure explorer remains confined to construction and can visually see the room but cannot leave the tabletop unless explicitly designed.

**Do not source** by scraping proprietary Studio/LDD/Mecabricks/BrickLink asset payloads, marketplace previews, LEGO video-game assets or a random Sketchfab model without exact redistributable license and clear authorship. Do not put Nova runtime code into the shipped game without an intentional license decision. Check Quaternius *current* licence rather than old listings claiming CC0.

### Download, bundle and audit contract (mandatory)

1. Create `game/assets/sourced/{kenney_bricks,ldraw,room,materials}/`, `game/assets/licenses/` and `game/assets/provenance.json` (exact names can match existing convention); fetch real files from publisher URLs and record release ID, source URL, retrieval date, SHA-256 of source ZIP and individual converted assets, author, SPDX licence, required attribution text, dependency tree, conversion command/tool version, normalized stud transforms, and reviewed verdict. Bundle the **approved runtime meshes/textures** in all offline Web/Android releases; no CDN dependency.
2. Keep source files where allowed and practical; for LDraw include dependency closure and attribution notice. When a licence is incompatible with redistributing raw pack in this public repo, **do not commit it**; record exclusion and substitute CC0 source. Never assume a zip licence extends to external textures/downloads.
3. Import real glTF/GLB/OBJ/DAT-origin geometry, do not approximate a specialty part with generic `BoxMesh`/cylinders when a matching approved mesh exists. Generic procedural *collision proxies, connector metadata, and baseplate shell* are allowed; procedural replacement **visual geometry of specialty bricks is not the target**. Existing placeholder IDs must map to reviewed meshes, with a clear exclusion/deprecation for unsupported shapes.
4. Make a contact sheet/grid per pack with ID, source model name, white/colored/perspective silhouettes and scale ruler. Minimum QA: **ship wheel** spokes/ring/hub, **curved slope** recognizable curve not wedge, **glass pane** translucent panel and compatible frame, arches actual underside opening, fish profile, fence gaps, round bricks round and studs aligned; also top/bottom/side/45° renders, shadow and transparent sorting. Preserve user saves by mapping old IDs.
5. Validate geometry against the canonical 20-LDU stud, 8-LDU plate and 24-LDU brick conventions as relevant (if this project uses a different world scale, map units consistently). Verify odd/even part pivot, 90° turns and multi-stud attachment numerically and visually. No visual-only fudging of stud origin, no physical collisions from elaborate imported mesh when a simplified collider suffices.
6. Catalog metadata must group **physical part families, shape ID, dimensions, connectors, source mesh ID, rarity, theme, visual material and thumbnail**; don't equate different colors with different geometry. Define curated starter hands: mostly straightforward 1×2/2×2/2×4 bricks and plates with occasional windows/doors, curves, foliage and play pieces, not random 1×12 + giant corner every turn. Seeded draws for tests, weighted variety in play, no impossible-only or nonsensical hands.
7. A family-facing credits/licence page and generated notice file must be verifiable from the provenance manifest. Open-source *code* and bundled *assets* can use different licences; keep both explicit.

## Camera and input contract (LB-069)

| Context | Desktop | Touch | Must happen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Move held part | pointer moves valid ghost on plate | one-finger drag on plate or tap | snap preview follows actual hit; never camera motion |
| Orbit | right-drag or explicit Camera tool + left-drag | two-finger horizontal/vertical drag, or visible Camera tool | rotate target round plate; **no placement** |
| Pan laterally | middle-drag, Shift+right-drag or tool set explicitly to Pan | two-finger drag in Pan mode or drag navigation puck | move actual camera target sideways on tabletop plane, not just orbit |
| Zoom | wheel over canvas with UI hover excluded, +/- optional | pinch | maintain focus under cursor / plate, bounded, no part placement |
| Placement | Enter or prominent Place button when valid; optional unambiguous tap-to-commit mode | dedicated thumb-reachable Place, or explicit tap-to-commit setting | one accepted event, haptic/click exactly once |
| Escape / reset | Escape releases piece, F frames plate, Home resets view | visible Cancel / Frame icons | never loses turn or existing build |

Double-click can recenter on an inspected board location, but **must not be the only way to pan**. While a part is selected, camera gestures keep the piece suspended/pinned without silently placing it. Changing camera mode needs distinct active state and actual labels `Orbit`, `Pan`, `Place`; no cycling label suggesting Pan while gesture is merely turning camera off. Camera/pointer interaction logic must work with modals, UI trays, resizing, high-DPI, web pointer capture and Android multi-touch. Respect reduced motion.
